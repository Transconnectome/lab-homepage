Awe and autobiographical recall can involve several emotions that change over time. We combine virtual reality (VR), EEG, and experience reports to study these complex emotions alongside brain activity.

### Awe and mixed feelings

Our [study of awe](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00299-2) measured emotional changes and EEG while participants watched VR videos. Ambivalent feelings, in which positive and negative affect coexist, were related to the intensity of awe. EEG analyses also identified corresponding patterns. The findings suggest that awe cannot be adequately described as a purely positive emotion.

### Emotion during recall and content generation

[Revisiting Your Memory](https://doi.org/10.1145/3746277.3760413) is an exploratory study that estimates emotional changes from EEG during recall and uses them to guide music and video generation. The memory content comes from participants' own writing, drawings, and music. EEG-derived emotional changes help control the generation process; the system does not reconstruct remembered scenes from brain signals alone.

### Visual and musical styles

We also develop generative models that change the style of images and music. [AesFA](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i12.29232) separates image features into frequency components to transfer style while accounting for texture and overall structure. Our [music style-transfer study](https://doi.org/10.1109/icip61757.2026.11630156) represents sound as a time–frequency image and adapts an image diffusion model to change musical style without additional training.
